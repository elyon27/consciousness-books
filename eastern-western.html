ABOUT THE BOOK

How do different traditions understand the self and awareness?

A comparative exploration of consciousness across philosophical and cultural traditions. The book introduces Indian and Chinese thought alongside classical Greek philosophy, Cartesian dualism, and contemporary approaches to mind and experience.

Its central interest is dialogue: where different traditions meet, where they disagree, and how translation, cultural assumptions, and methods of inquiry shape understanding. Meditation, contemplation, scientific investigation, and ethical reflection are considered as different contributions to an enduring question.

THE CENTRAL IDEAS

What you will explore

  • Selfhood and the mind-body relationship
  • Contemplative and empirical methods
  • Cultural translation and interpretation
  • Shared questions and distinct traditions

INSIDE THE BOOK

A chapter-by-chapter guide

Chapter titles follow the supplied edition; descriptions summarize their subjects.

  1. Introduction

    Sets out the purpose of examining consciousness across cultures.

  2. Historical Background

    Introduces Vedanta, Samkhya, Yoga, Taoism, Confucianism, Buddhism, Greek thought, and Western philosophical developments.

  3. Comparative Analysis

    Examines ideas about unity, selfhood, contemplation, subjectivity, objectivity, and the mind-body relationship.

  4. Contemporary Views

    Considers neuroscience, consciousness theories, and the encounter between mindfulness and Western psychology.

  5. Challenges and Questions

    Explores translation difficulties, cultural assumptions, and ethical differences.

  6. Future Directions

    Proposes interdisciplinary collaboration, cultural exchange, and more inclusive inquiry.

  7. Conclusion

    Draws together the case for respectful dialogue without erasing meaningful differences.

READING THIS VOLUME

Who it is for

Readers drawn to comparative philosophy, cultural studies, spirituality, and the diverse histories of ideas about mind.

A thoughtful reading approach

Read this volume as an educational and philosophical exploration. Distinguish observations, interpretations, and hypotheses as you follow the discussion.
