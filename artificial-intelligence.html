ABOUT THE BOOK

Could a machine process information without experiencing anything?

This volume brings the philosophical question of consciousness into conversation with artificial intelligence. It introduces human awareness, traces the development of AI, and asks what would be required to describe a machine as conscious rather than simply capable.

The discussion extends beyond technical performance to human identity, ethical responsibility, employment, privacy, and the social consequences of increasingly sophisticated systems. The book advocates responsible innovation and a continuing dialogue between technology and the humanities.

INSIDE THE BOOK

A chapter-by-chapter guide

Chapter titles follow the supplied edition; descriptions summarize their subjects.

  1. Introduction

    Defines consciousness and introduces its significance for human experience and AI.

  2. Historical Perspectives on Consciousness

    Traces philosophical views, scientific investigation, and the evolution of artificial intelligence.

  3. Understanding Consciousness

    Surveys competing accounts of awareness, subjectivity, and cognition.

  4. Current State of Artificial Intelligence

    Introduces AI capabilities and limitations as presented in the 2025 edition.

  5. Consciousness in AI

    Examines arguments, possible criteria, and objections concerning machine consciousness.

  6. Ethical Implications

    Discusses responsibility, rights, autonomy, and the human consequences of AI.

  7. Technological Advancements

    Considers emerging approaches and interfaces between brains and computational systems.

  8. Future Prospects

    Explores possible developments and the importance of human-centered design.

  9. Critiques and Debates

    Presents disagreements and challenges to claims about conscious machines.

  10. Conclusion

    Calls for interdisciplinary reflection and responsible AI development.

READING THIS VOLUME

Who it is for

Read this volume as an educational and philosophical exploration. Distinguish observations, interpretations, and hypotheses as you follow the discussion.
