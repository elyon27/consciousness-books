ABOUT THE BOOK

How does the brain relate to the experience of being aware?

An accessible introduction to the biological side of conscious experience. This book follows the relationship between neural activity and subjective awareness, moving from the basic organization of the nervous system to the tools used to investigate the conscious brain.

Rather than treating consciousness as a single location in the brain, the discussion considers interacting neural processes, competing theoretical frameworks, and the difficulty of connecting objective measurements with first-person experience. Its closing emphasis is on collaboration across neuroscience, psychology, and philosophy.

THE CENTRAL IDEAS

What you will explore

  • Neural activity and subjective experience
  • Brain imaging and its limitations
  • Integrated Information Theory and Global Workspace Theory
  • First-person and third-person approaches

INSIDE THE BOOK

A chapter-by-chapter guide

Chapter titles follow the supplied edition; descriptions summarize their subjects.

  1. Introduction

    Introduces consciousness, its historical background, and contemporary theoretical approaches.

  2. Neuroscience Fundamentals

    Explains neurons, synapses, brain structures, neurotransmitters, and the networks involved in cognition.

  3. Brain Mapping Techniques

    Introduces methods such as EEG, fMRI, and PET, and considers what each can reveal about brain activity.

  4. Neural Correlates of Consciousness (NCC)

    Examines the neural processes associated with conscious experience and the challenge of interpreting correlations.

  5. Altered States of Consciousness

    Considers sleep, meditation, and other changes in awareness as windows into brain function.

  6. Advancements in Consciousness Research

    Explores developments in technology, analysis, and theoretical models.

  7. Challenges and Future Directions

    Addresses unresolved questions, methodological limits, ethical issues, and interdisciplinary research.

  8. Conclusion

    Brings together the biological and experiential perspectives and calls for further investigation.
