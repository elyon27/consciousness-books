ABOUT THE BOOK

Where do physical explanation and the mystery of experience meet?

This book examines the conceptual meeting point between consciousness and quantum physics. It introduces foundational quantum ideas before turning to philosophical accounts of mind and historical debates about observers, measurement, and reality.

It discusses figures including Niels Bohr, Eugene Wigner, and David Bohm, as well as proposals such as the Orch-OR model associated with Roger Penrose and Stuart Hameroff. Questions about evidence, experimental difficulty, and criticism are part of the book’s exploration, alongside broader philosophical and ethical implications.

THE CENTRAL IDEAS

What you will explore

  • Historical debates about the observer
  • Orch-OR and competing explanations

READING THIS VOLUME

Who it is for

Readers who enjoy the philosophy of science and want an exploratory introduction to debates about mind and quantum theory.

A thoughtful reading approach

Read this volume as an educational and philosophical exploration. Distinguish observations, interpretations, and hypotheses as you follow the discussion.

Quantum consciousness proposals are presented as debated ideas. Quantum physics alone does not establish that consciousness creates reality; Integrated Information Theory is not inherently a quantum theory.
